Internet Terminology

Extranet

An extranet is a controlled private network that allows external parties — such as business partners, suppliers, or customers — limited, authenticated access to an organisation's intranet resources.

What is an Extranet?

An extranet extends an organisation's intranet to selected external users, granting them controlled access to specific resources. It sits between the fully private intranet and the fully public Internet. Common examples include supplier portals, client project spaces, franchise management systems, and partner collaboration platforms.

How Extranets Work

Extranets typically use VPN (Virtual Private Network) tunnels, HTTPS, or dedicated leased lines to connect external parties securely. Users authenticate with credentials; access is restricted to only the resources they need. The connection may be point-to-point between two organisations' networks or a web-based portal.

Intranet vs Extranet vs Internet

The Internet is public and open to all. An intranet is private and only for internal employees. An extranet is semi-private — it opens selected intranet resources to specific, trusted external parties under controlled conditions, bridging internal and external collaboration.

Business Use Cases

Retailers share inventory systems with suppliers. Law firms share case documents with clients. Manufacturers give distributors access to product catalogues and order systems. Healthcare organisations share records with partner clinics. Airlines connect with travel agencies for real-time booking systems.

Security Challenges

Extranets introduce security risks by expanding the network perimeter. Best practices include strong authentication (MFA), fine-grained access control, encrypted connections, regular security audits, session timeouts, and prompt removal of access when partnerships end.
